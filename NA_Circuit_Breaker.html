Governing principle A professional decline is not a refusal to work — it is a statement of capacity reality. If the hierarchy chooses to override, the risk of what is displaced transfers with that decision.
Request arrives
Step 1 · Assess
Classify against priority tier
Map the request against the published RACI. Is it P1 (core remit), P2 (7-day SLA), or P3 (unfunded)?
Consider: does it displace something already in flight?
P1 — within remit
Step 2a · Proceed
Accept and schedule
P1 work is absorbed into normal delivery. Log it in the register as standard. No escalation required.
P2 or P3 — outside capacity
Step 2b · Decline
Issue a documented decline
Decline the request in writing (email is sufficient). State:

1. The priority tier and why it falls outside current capacity.
2. What is currently in flight that would be displaced.
3. That the request remains open if the requestor wishes to escalate.

Tone: professional and factual. No apology required. Log the decline in the register.
No escalation
Step 3a · Close
Decline stands
The matter closes. The requestor accepts the position. Log as closed — declined, not escalated. This is evidence of professional capacity management.
|
Escalation raised
Step 3b · Escalate
Manager conversation
Your manager will engage. Present the RACI, the current in-flight work, and the specific displacement. You are defending a documented position — not resisting work.
Step 4 · Override
Manager instructs you to proceed
Acknowledge the instruction. Confirm in writing (reply to the email chain or follow-up note) the specific work that will be displaced as a result. You are not seeking permission — you are creating a record.

Example: "Understood. To confirm, proceeding with [X] means [Y] will slip beyond its current commitment. Please let me know if that changes the instruction."
Manager confirms · Risk transfers
Step 5 · Log & Proceed
Register the override and displaced work
Log the override, the instruction source, and the displaced deliverable in the reprioritisation register. Proceed with the overridden work. The risk of the displaced item is now owned by the person who gave the instruction.

What displacement looks like in practice — examples
P3 forced to P1 FQDN audit demanded urgently. Deviation Register review (P1) slips. New deviations go unassessed — regulatory exposure accumulates.
P2 volume overwhelms P1 Five concurrent POC reviews (P2) consume the week. ARB submission (P1) misses governance gate. Programme is delayed one full sprint cycle.
Project demand absorbs strategy time Embedded project resource (P3) consumes three days. Federated governance framework update (P1) stalls. Downstream teams work to stale patterns.
Informal ask becomes formal blocker Informal "can you just look at this" (P3) expands. Capability matrix review (P1) is deferred. Headcount planning cycle proceeds on inaccurate data.
